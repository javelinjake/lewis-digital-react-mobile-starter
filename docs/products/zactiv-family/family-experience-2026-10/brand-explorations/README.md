# Two new family health and fitness identities

Initial brand and app concepts · 2 October 2026

[Open the comparison gallery](index.html). These are original visual directions for Family Move & Bloom and Family in Motion. They replace the previous logo and colour identity in these concept boards; the production app and earlier explorations remain unchanged.

## Family Move & Bloom

[Design board](family-move-and-bloom.png)

Warm, encouraging family wellbeing with movement at its centre. The logo direction uses two abstract reaching figures that suggest a leaf; the interface keeps the botanical idea subtle so the product still reads as health and fitness.

| Colour | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Forest | `#214E45` | Wordmark, headings and primary buttons with white text |
| Sage | `#CCDCCE` | Supporting surfaces and selected-category backgrounds |
| Rose | `#D68C99` | Small decorative accents with dark text where necessary |
| Pink white | `#FBF7F8` | Page background |
| Charcoal green | `#253932` | Body text |

Typography: a confident rounded humanist sans, warm without looking like a toddler product. Illustration: family movement in familiar rooms, including seated participation. Brand phrase: **Move together. Grow well.**

The three screen concepts show a short movement suggestion, an adapted stretching activity and a private family record that also accommodates food and feeling good. No weekly requirement or disappearing progress.

Motion intent: gentle 180ms card transitions and a small one-shot acknowledgement. Avoid a growing plant that wilts when a family stops using the app. The word Bloom refers to wellbeing, not performance rewards.

Risk to test: does the nature language make this seem like gardening or general lifestyle? Keep movement imagery and clear family fitness/wellbeing wording prominent.

## Family in Motion

[Design board](family-in-motion.png)

A more energetic active-family identity. The mark direction uses three rounded ascending strokes suggesting movement and people, rather than a conventional gym symbol.

| Colour | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Plum | `#482C53` | Wordmark, headings and primary buttons with white text |
| Lavender | `#DED5EB` | Supporting surfaces |
| Citron | `#D5E75A` | Energetic highlights with dark text |
| Cloud | `#F6F5FA` | Page background |
| Charcoal | `#302A38` | Body text |

Typography: bold, slightly condensed headings with a readable humanist body. Illustration: contemporary editorial family activity with curved movement accents. Brand phrase: **Move together. Feel good together.**

The three screens show a five-minute family movement break, two suitable activity choices and a calm end to a session. Move, Eat well and Feel good remain visible parts of the offer. No scores, calorie counts or required next workout.

Motion intent: a short 120–180ms action response and a single optional curved-line reveal after an attempt. No continuously animated rings, countdown urgency or performance charts. Reduce motion support is required in implementation.

Risk to test: does the energetic identity make beginners expect harder workouts? Pair it with your-pace wording, gentle/low-impact options and welcoming movement illustrations.

## Compare the direction, not the illustration alone

Move & Bloom has a softer wellbeing balance; Family in Motion makes the active purpose more immediate. Both can support movement, food and emotional wellbeing. Both should show a useful suggestion first and let a family stop without penalty.

The logo marks are initial generated concepts, not final vector assets or cleared trademarks. The boards were created with the built-in imagegen tool; exact prompts are saved in [prompts.json](prompts.json). Raster colours/lettering are illustrative: these hex values and the written specifications are the implementation reference. No font files were acquired or licensed for these new identities.

These boards are static design ideas. They do not implement workouts, animation, accounts or saved household data. Before choosing a direction, test what parents think the app does from its first screen and whether the name/art feels welcoming to less-active families.
