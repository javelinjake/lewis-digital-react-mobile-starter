# Portfolio scaling by re-theming one game template (sports and non-sport siblings)

Research date: 25 November 2026. Figures are labelled **[company]** (company-reported), **[estimate]** (third-party app-intelligence estimate) or **[press]** (journalist statement without a stated data source). Most evidence came from search-result summaries and single-page fetches; where I could not open the primary page I say so. Several of the named studios (Full Fat, Nordeus, Tapps, From The Bench, Metamoki, Gamevil) returned no usable numbers and are listed under Gaps rather than padded out.

## 1. Case studies: studios that reused one framework across sports/themes

### Takeaway
The best-documented direct analogue (New Star Games: Retro Bowl -> Retro Goal) shows a sibling on the same base earning a small fraction of the original (about 2% of revenue and 13% of downloads at the one comparable point in 2021), and the studio's later growth came from deepening the hit (college edition, NFL-licensed Apple Arcade edition), not from more sports. Template portfolios do work at volume (Kairosoft, Kolibri) but revenue is concentrated in one or two titles and the long tail earns little per game.

### Cited Findings

**New Star Games (UK; New Star Soccer -> Retro Bowl -> Retro Goal -> Retro Bowl College -> NFL Retro Bowl)**

- [press] Retro Bowl's breakout was tied to App Store featuring and TikTok videos in September 2021, i.e. an organic/viral event, not portfolio cross-promotion. — [PocketGamer.biz, 9 Feb 2023](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/f2p-scores-a-win-with-retro-goals-monetisation-strategy-changes/)

### Inferences
- Retro Goal / Retro Bowl at Nov 2021: $67K / $3M = about 2.2% of revenue and 431K / 3.4M = about 12.7% of downloads. Even allowing for Retro Goal being five months old versus 22 months, a football (soccer) re-theme of an American-football hit by the same studio, in a sport the studio was already famous for, did not transfer the audience. The hit was driven by a TikTok/featuring moment specific to one title.
- New Star's revealed preference after that result was depth and brand extension inside the winning sport (college, licensed NFL edition, annual Apple Arcade releases, console ports), not breadth across sports.
- Kolibri and Kairosoft show the template model is sustainable when (a) the first title is already a durable hit that funds the rest and (b) each sibling adds some real mechanical novelty. In both, one title dominates.
- No source gave evidence that cross-promotion between sibling sports titles materially moved installs. I found no published cross-promo conversion figures for any of the named studios.

### Gaps
- No download or revenue figures found for Hothead's individual Big Win titles, Full Fat's Flick series, Nordeus, Miniclip's individual sports titles, Tapps Games, From The Bench, Com2uS/Gamevil sibling lines, Metamoki, or Pixel Cup Soccer. Sensor Tower/AppMagic paid dashboards would be needed.
- No company-reported revenue for New Star Games: the Companies House filing itself was not opened, and the filing type implies no P&L. Balance-sheet reserves movement could be read from the filed accounts as a proxy for retained profit.
- No sales comparison for Retro Bowl College vs Retro Bowl was found.
- No published cross-promotion effectiveness data for any sports portfolio.

## 2. Store policy: Apple guideline 4.3 and Google Play repetitive content

### Takeaway
Both stores explicitly prohibit the pattern "same app, different sports team/theme, separate listings"; Apple's own text names "sports teams" and tells developers to ship one app with variations via in-app purchase. A sibling needs visibly different rules, content and presentation, not just art and copy, and should come from one developer account.

### Cited Findings
- Apple 4.3(a), verbatim: "Don't create multiple Bundle IDs of the same app ... If your app has different versions for specific locations, sports teams, universities, etc., consider submitting a single app and providing the variations using in-app purchase." — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple 4.3(b), verbatim: "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available. Opportunistically creating variants of existing app categories or popular apps degrades App Store discovery ... Repeated submissions of this kind may lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program." — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple 4.2.6: apps created from a commercialised template or app-generation service are rejected unless submitted directly by the provider of the app's content. (Targets bought templates, not an in-house shared codebase.) — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple's rejection wording as reported by developers: the app "shares a similar binary, metadata, and/or concept as other apps submitted to the App Store with only minor differences". An Apple reply lists buying a third-party template and submitting similar apps across multiple accounts as common causes. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 842971](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/842971?answerId=902713022); [thread 773504](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/773504)
- Enforcement anecdotes (2025-2026 forum threads, self-reported): one developer rejected five times despite major code and content changes with the appeal upheld against them; another iterated 20+ versions without clearing 4.3; some were approved on re-review; a developer of 15+ years reported a first-ever 4.3 rejection. Forum advice is to ask review whether the finding is 4.3(a) (duplication among your own apps) or 4.3(b) (insufficient differentiation from the store), and to demonstrate differences concretely. — [thread 843096](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/843096); [thread 850731](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/850731); [thread 787133](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/787133); [thread 774870](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/774870)
- Google Play Spam policy, Repetitive Content, verbatim: "We don't allow apps that merely provide the same experience as other apps already on Google Play. Apps should provide value to users through the creation of unique content or services." Listed violations include "Creating multiple apps with highly similar functionality, content, and user experience"; remedy suggested: "developers should consider creating a single app that aggregates all the content." — [Google Play Developer Policy: Spam](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en)
- Google's enforcement is partly automated and has flagged one developer's similar-looking variant apps (icon packs) as repetitive; the policy dates from 2018 and its definition is short. — [PhoneArena](https://phonearena.com/news/Google-Play-Stores-spam-policies-are-heavily-undefined-and-here-is-the-perfect-example_id145418)

### Inferences
- Legitimate series that coexist on the stores (Retro Bowl / Retro Goal / Retro Bowl College; Kairosoft's catalogue; Kolibri's tycoons) differ in core on-pitch or core-loop mechanics, not only in theme. Retro Goal has a different match engine from Retro Bowl. This is an observation from the catalogue, not a stated Apple rule.
- For Pixel Pitch's factory, the risk is highest where the sibling keeps the identical pack/lineup/idle shell and only swaps sport nouns and sprites: that is close to Apple's literal "sports teams" example and Google's "highly similar functionality, content, and user experience". A different mini-game set and a sport-specific simulation in the `core` rules package is the natural differentiator; a shared binary fingerprint (same framework, same web bundle structure) is a plausible but unconfirmed trigger for the "similar binary" wording.
- Apple's suggested alternative (one app, sports as in-app variations) is a real product option: a single multi-sport "Pixel" club app avoids 4.3 entirely and concentrates ratings and live-ops, at the cost of separate store listings and keyword surface.

### Gaps
- No documented 2023-2026 case of a named sports-game series being removed under 4.3 or Google's repetitive-content policy was found; evidence is developer forum anecdotes only.
- Neither store publishes thresholds for "similar binary"; nobody outside review knows how similarity is measured.
- Google's "Functionality, Content and User Experience" page (minimum functionality) was not retrieved.

## 3. Audience size and spend by sport

### Takeaway
Sports is a mid-sized, flat-to-declining mobile genre dominated by licensed incumbents; football (soccer) is the largest single sport (about 28% of sports-game revenue in commercial market reports) and the only one with a measured 2026 tailwind. I could not find a reliable sport-by-sport revenue table from a primary data provider; the per-title datapoints below are scattered and of different ages.

### Cited Findings
- [estimate] AppMagic, full-year 2025: Sports Games about 845.8M downloads (-2.5% YoY) and $257.5M revenue (-8.7% YoY) in one table; the same report family also shows a sports row of 2.1B downloads and $1.7B revenue. Scope definitions differ (likely casual-sports sub-taxonomy vs all sports) and the discrepancy is unresolved. — [AppMagic Mobile Market Landscape 2026 (PDF)](https://appmagic.rocks/files/view/upload/Reports/EN_MobileMarkeLandscape2026.pdf); [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-market-landscape)

- [estimate] Total mobile game revenue grew only 0.2% in 2025 while downloads grew 4.6% (AppMagic). — [Prism News summary of AppMagic](https://www.prismnews.com/news/appmagic-mobile-games-revenue-grew-just-02-in-2025)
- [low-quality market-research summaries] Football/soccer is put at about 28.4-28.5% of mobile sports game revenue in 2025; one report groups baseball and tennis at 27% combined. Market-size figures from these vendors conflict wildly ($12.8B vs $25.5B for 2025) and methodology is undisclosed; use the share only as a rough ordering. — [Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/global-mobile-sports-game-spg-market); [WiseGuy Reports](https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/mobile-sports-game-market)

- **Golf:** [estimate] Golf Clash reached about $780-789M lifetime revenue on about 64M downloads (data.ai/Sensor Tower; figures several years old), topping US player spending in sports; EA's acquisition announcement cited 80M+ downloads. Naavik notes franchise-led titles such as Madden earn far less per download than Golf Clash. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/76758/golf-clash-has-scored-780-million-in-lifetime-revenue-tops-us-player-spending/); [Naavik: Golf Clash vs Golf Rival](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/the-turf-war-between-golf-clash-golf-rival/)

### Inferences
- Revenue per download differs by an order of magnitude between sports: baseball (MLB 9 Innings about $9.6) and golf (Golf Clash about $12 on the 64M figure) sit far above viral casual American football (Retro Bowl under $1 per download on the 2021 AppMagic figures) and above cricket, where installs are huge and India-weighted. These ratios are my arithmetic on the cited figures, which come from different years and methods.
- High-ARPU baseball and golf audiences are older, US/Japan/Korea-weighted and served by licensed or physics-skill incumbents; a casual pixel card-collection game is not an obvious fit for their spending pattern, and baseball spend is strongly tied to real-player licences.
- For a UK solo developer whose existing game is unlicensed pixel football, the sports where an unlicensed casual title has demonstrably worked are American football (Retro Bowl) and football (New Star Soccer); I found no equivalent proof for hockey, rugby, tennis, darts, F1 or combat sports.

### Gaps
- No reliable per-sport revenue or download breakdown from Sensor Tower, AppMagic or data.ai was publicly accessible. The Sensor Tower Sports Report 2026 has a "Sports Gaming" section that is behind the download gate.
- No figures found for NBA 2K Mobile, Madden Mobile, F1 Clash, Cricket League (Miniclip), or for ice hockey, tennis, rugby, darts, wrestling/combat sports, or motorsport game audiences.
- No data found on which sports have "underserved casual/collection niches"; any such claim would be opinion.
- No Philippines/China basketball or Japan/Korea baseball mobile data retrieved.

## 4. Non-sport re-themes of the pack-collecting + idle loop

### Takeaway
The card-collection revenue at the top of the market is driven by globally loved IP with an existing physical-collecting culture (Pokémon), not by the loop itself; the one cited original-mechanic licensed comparator (Marvel Snap) earns a tiny fraction and is declining. I found no evidence that an unlicensed theme swap (fantasy, monsters, racing, music) monetises a pack loop better than sport.

### Cited Findings
- [estimate] Pokémon TCG Pocket: $1.6bn in 1.5 years per PocketGamer.biz coverage (2026); a separate AppMagic-based figure gives $975.2M net revenue in two years, led by Japan. The two differ (gross vs net and/or method). Over $1.3bn in its first year, overtaking Pokémon GO's debut. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-tcg-pocket-makes-16bn-in-15-years/); [PocketGamer.biz, second anniversary](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokmon-trading-card-game-pocket-celebrates-second-anniversary/); [GamerBraves](https://www.gamerbraves.com/pokemon-tcg-pocket-surpasses-1-3-billion-in-first-year-revenue-overtaking-pokemon-go/)
- [estimate] Pokémon TCG Pocket passed 100 million downloads by March 2026 (Sensor Tower). — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/pokemon-tcg-pocket-100-million-downloads)
- [company, DeNA results] Pokémon TCG Pocket users fell from 39 million to 23 million year on year in Q1 2026; outlets disagree on whether this is a daily or monthly metric. DeNA reported lower game revenue and profit as a result. — [Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/pokemon-trading-card-game/pocket-losing-players); [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/huge-pokemon-tcg-pocket-user-drop-revealed-in-financial-report/)
- [estimate, Naavik using Sensor Tower] Marvel Snap was "stagnating" at about $4.711M monthly revenue and 2.38M active users (article date unclear). Naavik attributes Pocket's revenue partly to a "massive collector-driven fanbase in Japan". — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/pokemon-tcg-collects-some-new-tricks/)
- [estimate] Pokémon's mobile portfolio totals $10.7bn lifetime. — [PocketGamer.biz, Pokémon Day 2026](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-day-2026-legendaries-legacies-and-the-latest-opportunities-for-a-107bn-mobile-portfolio)
- [estimate] Idle/tycoon themes on a shared template: Kolibri's bank and firefighter themes each earned about $3M+; a Codigames university theme earns under $10K/month on about 3M installs. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/game-analysis-deconstructing-idle-bank-tycoon-by-kolibri-games); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.codigames.university.empire.idle.tycoon)

### Inferences
- Pack-opening monetises when the cards themselves carry meaning to the buyer before the game exists (Pokémon; real athletes in Ultimate Team-style modes). Fictional pixel players or original monsters lack that pre-existing desire, so a non-sport re-theme competes on charm and game feel, where the template gives no special advantage.
- Even with the strongest IP in the category, a pure collection loop lost roughly 40% of its users in a year, which suggests collection alone needs constant content to hold players, a live-ops cost that multiplies with each sibling title.

### Gaps
- No comparative data on which unlicensed themes (fantasy, monsters, anime-style, racing, music) monetise best in card-collection or idle RPGs was found; I did not retrieve idle-RPG genre revenue.
- No case study of a sports card game successfully re-themed to a non-sport setting on the same template.

## 5. Licensing: real names, image rights, and how unlicensed games cope

### Takeaway
Real player names and likenesses are protected and have been litigated successfully even against EA; unlicensed games survive by using fictional names and city-not-club naming, and the Pixel Pitch approach (fictional players) is the safe one. A licence is a route for a proven hit, as New Star's NFL deal came four years after Retro Bowl broke out.

### Cited Findings
- Apple 5.2.1: "Don't use protected third-party material such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas in your app without permission ... Apps should be submitted by the person or legal entity that owns or has licensed the intellectual property". — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Retro Bowl has no NFL licence, no real teams and fake player names; it uses the city name in place of the team name with a similar jersey. Real team names and colours could be unlocked (reported at $1.99) but not real players; a Switch review noted player names could not be changed (may be outdated). — [Wikipedia: Retro Bowl](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Retro_Bowl); [Awful Announcing, 1 Apr 2026](https://awfulannouncing.com/gaming/while-ea-fights-over-nfl-licenses-retro-bowl-is-winning-football-gaming-without-one.html); [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/084459/retro-bowl-three-reasons-why-you-might-like-the-american-football-game/)
- The licensed version is a separate product: NFL Retro Bowl '25 launched on Apple Arcade in 2024 with a full NFL licence, while the original stays unlicensed and free. — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/nfl-retro-bowl-25-new-nfl-licensed-arcade-game-releasing-ahead-of-2024-season)
- US rights are split: the NFLPA group licence covers player names and likenesses; the NFL licence covers team names, uniforms and marks. A small studio (Bit Fry) obtained an NFLPA licence through OneTeam Partners, and Football Heroes: Pro Edition (2014) shipped real players without team names. — [NFLPA licensing](https://nflpa.com/partners/licensing); [NFLPA: Bit Fry announcement](https://nflpa.com/partners/posts/bit-fry-game-studios-announces-licensing-agreement-with-oneteam-partners-and-the-nflpa); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/new-football-game-will-use-real-player-names-despite-m-1583834047)
- FIFPro acquires player image rights via unions in nearly 60 countries and makes them available to EA and other game clients; of Europe's top five leagues only Serie A has not collectivised video-game rights. — [Fordham IPLJ, 11 Nov 2021](https://fordhamiplj.org/2021/11/11/fifa-how-does-the-most-successful-sports-video-game-obtain-player-i-rights/); [Lewis Silkin](https://sportsinsights.lewissilkin.com/post/102gl2f/image-rights-in-the-spotlight-again-as-zlatan-fires-shot-at-ea-sports-and-fifpro)
- Even licensed use has been challenged: a Hamburg court (April 2003) barred EA from selling FIFA World Cup 2002 in Germany over Oliver Kahn's likeness, with fines up to €250,000, because Kahn was outside the FIFPro/Bundesliga licences. — [Pinsent Masons Out-Law, 30 Apr 2003](https://pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/football-game-banned-over-players-image-rights)
- A Brazilian court awarded R$6.5M (R$5,000 per player per game) to 450+ players over EA's use of their images in 2005-2014, holding that image rights belong to the athlete and FIFPro could not assign them. — [SBS News](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/brazilian-club-players-sue-over-video-game-image-rights/eai9g9ywe)
- In 2020 Zlatan Ibrahimović and Gareth Bale publicly objected to the use of their names and likenesses in FIFA. — [The Week](https://theweek.com/gaming/108772/zlatan-vs-ea-sports-image-rights-fifa-video-game)

### Inferences
- If EA with FIFPro and league licences loses image-rights cases, an indie using real names with no licence has no defence; store takedown under 5.2.1 is the likelier first consequence than a lawsuit.
- City or nation names plus original kit colours and fictional players is the established safe pattern. Community-editable names shift the act to the user but I found no legal authority confirming that this protects the developer.
- Each new sport adds a new rights landscape (leagues, unions, governing bodies, event names such as "World Cup", "Super Bowl", "IPL", "Olympics"), so the legal review cost is per sport and does not get cheaper with a shared codebase.

### Gaps
- No pricing found for small/indie licences (FIFPro, OneTeam/NFLPA, individual clubs or leagues); minimum guarantees are not published.
- No UK-specific authority retrieved on passing off / trade mark use of club names in games.
- No information found on how New Star Soccer or Pixel Cup Soccer handle naming, nor on the terms of New Star's NFL/Apple Arcade deal.
- No mobile-specific lawsuit over unlicensed player names was found.

## 6. Portfolio economics: marginal cost, live-ops burden, hit rate, depth vs breadth

### Takeaway
Industry commentary in 2025-26 points firmly toward depth: downloads are falling, players concentrate time in fewer games, and live ops "is the game". Template portfolios pay when a first hit exists to feed them; the measured sibling outcomes in section 1 suggest a second themed title should be expected to earn a fraction of the first.

### Cited Findings
- Deconstructor of Fun (mid-2025): total hours spent are roughly flat, revenue slightly up and downloads falling, i.e. players spend more time in fewer games. "Live ops is no longer a post-launch function. It's the game." — [Deconstructor of Fun: Mobile Gaming in 2025, 5 Jun 2025](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/6/5/the-state-of-mobile-gaming-2025)
- Deconstructor of Fun (Jan 2025): back catalogues of 2-5-year-old games are less attractive; players "rather engage with the latest events and seasons running in the top live games". — [Deconstructor of Fun: 6 Predictions for 2025](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/1/13/6-predictions-for-mobile-games-in-2025)
- Deconstructor of Fun: Chinese publishers win on execution, creative volume, live-ops cadence and iteration speed using centralised live-ops teams across portfolios; small teams competing head-on are "in an attrition war". — [Deconstructor of Fun: State of Gaming 2025 commentary](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/seven-things-the-2025-state-of-gaming-report-actually-tells-us)
- [estimate] Sensor Tower H1 2026: mobile game downloads fell about 12% to 24 billion. — [Sensor Tower H1 2026 Digital Gaming Market Index](https://sensortower.com/blog/h1-2026-digital-gaming-market-index)
- Product Madness' executive said it is only because the firm has a strong portfolio that it is able to create new games (older event write-up). — [MobileGamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/google-x-deconstructor-of-fun-in-istanbul-everything-you-need-to-know/)
- Hit-rate datapoints: 3 to 40+ prototypes per hit among Voodoo partner studios (self-reported, hyper-casual, where each prototype is days of work and is killed on early metrics). — [PocketGamer.biz: Tummy Games](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/77158/tummy-games-nemanjua-divjak-hypercasual-voodoo/); [PocketGamer.biz: Tarboosh Games](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-voodoo-approach-tarboosh-games/)
- Kolibri updated each live game weekly; Kairosoft staff each work across several titles. — [GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/making-a-hit-idle-game-eight-lessons-from-kolibri-games); [Apple App Store story](https://apps.apple.com/ca/story/id1484316385)
- Retro Bowl ran with zero UA spend and no ads (100% IAP), so a lean premium-style unlock model can carry a tiny team when organic reach exists. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/77425/uk-based-new-star-games-takes-us-by-storm-with-retro-bowl/); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.newstargames.retrobowl)

### Inferences
- The shared monorepo lowers build cost, which is the part of a sibling that was already cheapest. The costs that do not share are per-title: art and catalogue, store listing and ASO, sport-specific balance, legal review, customer support, season-pass content cadence, and above all acquiring an audience. With downloads falling 12% in H1 2026, audience acquisition is the binding constraint, not code.
- A season pass is a recurring content commitment. Two live games with season passes means two content calendars for one person; the cited commentary says cadence is where small teams lose.
- The factory's strongest use is as a cheap option, not a plan: ship sibling tests quickly, kill on early retention and store-conversion metrics as hyper-casual studios do, and keep only what clears a bar. That matches how the template studios actually reached their hits. But it only makes sense once Pixel Pitch itself shows retention and payer conversion worth copying; re-skinning an unproven loop multiplies an unknown.
- New Star's path suggests the higher-value second product is often an adjacent edition of the proven game (a different competition format or level of the same sport, or a platform deal such as Apple Arcade or a web portal like Poki) rather than a different sport.

### Gaps
- No published figure for the marginal cost of a second title on a shared codebase for a solo developer; no Naavik, GameDiscoverCo or GDC source specifically on solo-developer portfolio breadth was retrieved.
- No benchmark for how many concurrent live games one developer can sustain.
- Hit-rate data found is for hyper-casual prototypes only, not for mid-depth collection/idle games.

## 7. Seasonality and event hooks

### Takeaway
World Cup 2026 produced a real but concentrated lift: sports-game downloads in major Western markets rose about 54% across the tournament and 71% around the final, with revenue up only about 24%, and the gains went mostly to licensed leaders and well-timed releases. Events are a download hook more than a revenue hook, and the next one of comparable size for a UK football game is Euro 2028.

### Cited Findings
- [estimate, AppMagic via Mobidictum; US, UK, Germany, France, Italy, Spain; Sports Games category, not football-only; published 26 Jun 2026 so the "finals" figures may draw on prior-tournament patterns and the article does not give the data window] Sports-game downloads +54% throughout the tournament and +71% at the finals stage; net game revenue +24% at the finals. The article says the surge "was usually captured by genre leaders such as EA Sports FC Mobile or well-timed indie releases". — [Mobidictum, 26 Jun 2026](https://mobidictum.com/world-cup-2026-games-trends/)
- By contrast, sports (non-game) apps saw +297% downloads at the opening stage and +124% across the event. — [Mobidictum, 26 Jun 2026](https://mobidictum.com/world-cup-2026-games-trends/)
- [estimate, Sensor Tower] Global mobile game downloads were 3.64bn in June 2026 (-3.6% MoM) and 3.72bn in July 2026 (+2.2% MoM); Soccer Superstar, Football League 2026, EA SPORTS FC Mobile and eFootball all appeared in the July top 10 by downloads. — [Sensor Tower, June 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-june-2026); [Sensor Tower, July 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-july-2026)
- [estimate, Sensor Tower] Football titles were among the few mobile areas that grew in H1 2026 while overall downloads fell 12% to 24bn. — [Sensor Tower H1 2026 index](https://sensortower.com/blog/h1-2026-digital-gaming-market-index)
- [company, FIFA] FIFA Rivals, the official licensed arcade football game, passed 3.5 million downloads during the tournament. — [FIFA](https://inside.fifa.com/news/biggest-digital-football-activation-fifa-history-world-cup-2026)
- [estimate, Sensor Tower] Sports news/live-score apps hit a record 137M downloads in Q2 2026; India's share of FIFA Official App active users rose from 7% (2025) to 19% (H1 2026). — [Sensor Tower Sports Report 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/beyond-the-game-the-sports-report-2026)
- Football titles with World Cup tie-ins named in coverage: EA Sports FC Mobile 26, FIFA Heroes, eFootball 2026, Football Manager 26 Mobile, Dream League Soccer 2026, Top Eleven, Mini Football, and FIFA World Cup: Launch Edition (Netflix). — [Mobidictum, 26 Jun 2026](https://mobidictum.com/world-cup-2026-games-trends/)
- AppMagic has a standing analysis of football-season effects on mobile ("The FIFA Effect"); not opened. — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/fifa-effect-on-mobile-apps)

### Inferences
- Downloads rose roughly two to three times faster than revenue (+54/+71% vs +24%), so event traffic is lower-intent. For a game monetising via packs and a season pass, an event is best used to fill the top of the funnel, with retention doing the earning afterwards.
- The crowded list of licensed tie-ins shows that event windows are when competition for football keywords is fiercest; an unlicensed game cannot use the event's name or marks in its listing (Apple 5.2.1), which limits how much of the surge it can capture.
- Event hooks argue for football-first depth (Women's World Cup 2027, Euro 2028 in the UK and Ireland) at least as much as for new sports: Pixel Pitch already owns the relevant theme for the next two big dated events.

### Gaps
- No post-tournament (August 2026 or later) measured analysis isolating football-game downloads and revenue for World Cup 2026 was found; the best figures are category-level and published mid-tournament.
- No per-title uplift for small/unlicensed football games.
- No measured mobile-game effects retrieved for Super Bowl, IPL, the Olympics, or past Women's World Cups or Euros (beyond an unsourced UEFA Euro comparison in the Mobidictum piece).
