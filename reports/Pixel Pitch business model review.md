# Pixel Pitch needs retention before revenue

Pixel Pitch's business model is sound in shape but small in expected size: a season pass plus gems, with no paid loot boxes and no ads, is the cleanest and lowest-risk model a UK solo developer can ship in November 2026, and on the evidence it most likely earns tens to a few hundred pounds a month in its first year, not a salary. The base rates are blunt. Roughly 59% of monetising mobile games earn under $1,000 a month, only about 1.3% of 2025's new mobile games passed 500,000 downloads, and the closest stylistic comparable, Retro Goal, earned an estimated $67K in its first five months while its American-football twin earned $3M. This report's own model puts first-year net revenue at about **£190 (low), £2,900 (base) and £85,000 (high)**, and only the high case, which needs roughly 300,000 installs and top-decile retention, pays a living wage. The model's two real weaknesses are that it skips rewarded ads, which appear to supply most of the money in the pack-opener niche and a large share at the manager incumbents, and that a 28-day pass depends on day-30 retention, where the median game keeps under 1% of players. The technology choice is right: Capacitor with one shared codebase is a legitimate route for a card-and-idle game, and billing still runs through Apple and Google by default, with web shops now a legal second channel whose in-app promotion depends on platform and country. The app factory makes a second title cheap to build, yet the best-documented sibling experiment in this exact style returned about 2% of the original's revenue, and both stores explicitly prohibit same-app-different-sport listings. The next move is therefore a cheap soft launch that measures day-1, day-7 and day-30 retention, because every other decision (ads, pricing, paid acquisition, a publisher, a second sport) hinges on those three numbers.

A note on evidence before the detail. Company-reported figures are labelled as such. Third-party figures from AppMagic, Sensor Tower and AppGoblin are modelled estimates that can be wrong by a large factor for small apps; two reads of the same AppGoblin page for Retro Bowl returned "$10K+" and "$50K+" a month ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.newstargames.retrobowl)). Anything described as "this report's model", "assumption" or "inference" is the report's own reasoning and not a sourced fact. Product details come from the Pixel Pitch codebase.

## The model is fair and low-risk, but it leaves the niche's main revenue stream unused

### What the model gets right

Pixel Pitch sells a premium track on a 28-day League Pass and a hard currency (gems), earns nothing from ads, and has paid random packs built but switched off. That combination matches what the indie pixel-sports audience already accepts. Retro Bowl monetises with a £0.99 one-off unlock and credit packs from £0.99 to £29.99, with no ads ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/retro-bowl/id1478902583)); Retro Goal dropped its hard paywall in February 2023 for free career play plus an optional paid mode, still ad-free ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/f2p-scores-a-win-with-retro-goals-monetisation-strategy-changes/)); and Football Manager 26 Mobile markets "no ads or in-app purchases" as a feature on Netflix ([Netflix Tudum](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/football-manager-26-mobile-game-news)). A pass is also the monetisation format in the one casual segment still growing: hybrid-casual IAP rose about 20% in 2025 while the whole mobile games market grew 1.3% to $81.75bn ([GameDev Reports summary of Sensor Tower](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)).

Keeping paid random packs off is the single best decision in the design, and it is now worth more than goodwill. **For titles submitted from June 2026, PEGI gives any game with paid random items a minimum rating of 16**, defining them as "in-game offers to purchase digital goods or premiums where players don't know exactly what they are getting", with card packs named as an example ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/pegi-launches-interactive-risk-categories-overhauls-age-ratings-for-loot-boxes-in-game-spending-and-communication-features/)), and the rule covers purchases with virtual currency as well as real money ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114230/games-with-loot-boxes-now-automatically-rated-pegi-16-in-europe/index.html)). Apple requires odds disclosure for randomised items offered for purchase ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), the UK's ASA has enforced loot-box disclosure in mobile game ads and store listings since May 2026 ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/02/27/new-tough-measures-brought-in-by-the-asa-on-advertising-loot-boxes-in-the-uk-102mknq)), and Belgium has treated paid loot boxes as games of chance since 2018 ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/bel/publication/loot-boxes-a-treasure-trove-of-gambling-regulatory-issues)). A pixel football game rated 16 would lose much of its natural audience.

Two cautions follow, both inferences from the wording of those rules and not confirmed by any source. First, the definitions turn on purchase, so packs earned only by time, coins or tokens sit outside them, but the trap is the indirect route: if gems are sold for money and gems can reach random packs, that is a paid random item. In Pixel Pitch gems buy extra arcade plays, arcade plays earn tokens, and tokens buy arcade packs. A skill game sits in the middle of that chain, which is a real difference, but the developer should answer the age-rating questionnaire with that chain in mind and take advice if unsure. Second, the pass and the login calendar still trigger the lower PEGI tiers: rewards for returning carry PEGI 7 minimum and time-limited purchase offers PEGI 12 minimum ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/pegi-launches-interactive-risk-categories-overhauls-age-ratings-for-loot-boxes-in-game-spending-and-communication-features/)). Plan for a 7 to 12 rating.

### Where the model is weak

A pass converts well and caps spend. Most mobile passes cost $5 to $15 a month and return rewards worth 10 to 20 times the price, most of the revenue is the upfront purchase, and the format has "limited ARPPU potential" ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2022/6/4/battle-passes-analysis)). The cautionary case is Clash Royale, whose revenue per day trended down after its pass launched because collection depth capped out and the pass replaced other spending ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2020/7/16/8jsjtv3v25nfmw9x177ampscr836m0)). Google's own guidance found passes drive anywhere from 1% to 40% of a game's revenue depending on integration ([Google Play](https://goo.gle/3htI67y)). Pixel Pitch, with paid packs off and a finite collection, resembles the Clash Royale case: the only uncapped line is gems, and today gems have one sink (extra arcade plays at 20 gems). Whale revenue should be assumed negligible.

The pass also depends on players still being there on day 28. GameAnalytics' 2026 benchmarks across 16,262 games put **median day-30 retention at 0.69% to 0.79%** and the top quartile at 1.6% to 1.8% ([GameDev Reports summary of GameAnalytics](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). At the median almost nobody is present to buy a second season. And revenue arrives early: 38% of day-60 IAP revenue lands on install day and 60% by day 7 ([GameDev Reports summary of AppsFlyer](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appsflyer-app-monetization-in-2026)). A pass that is not purchasable and attractive in a player's first week forgoes most of the typical revenue window.

The largest gap is ads. The pack-opener apps closest to Pixel Pitch's loop monetise by advertising: MADFUT 26's UK listing shows "Contains Advertising" and no in-app purchases ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/madfut-26/id6752884808)). AppGoblin's modelled splits put Top Eleven at 61% ads and Online Soccer Manager at 77% ads ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/eu.nordeus.topeleven.android); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/400201466)), though those splits are estimates and an older Gamebasics case study said IAP was 75% of OSM's revenue ([AdMob case study, archived](https://swap.stanford.edu/was/20160311035101mp_/http://www.google.com/admob/pdf/admob-cs-gamebasics.pdf)). Idle games are the heaviest rewarded-video genre of all, at about 73 rewarded videos per user over lifetime in one vendor dataset ([App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/Mobile-Casual-Benchmarks-Report-2025/)). Pixel Pitch already has the natural placements (energy refills, shelf speed-ups, idle cap extensions) and currently sells them for gems only.

### Can it do well?

It can do well as a fair, well-reviewed small game, and it is unlikely to do well as a business on its own without more reach than a solo developer normally gets. No direct precedent exists for a no-ads, no-paid-packs, fictional-roster football collection game earning meaningful money from a pass. The honest framing is that the model is a good container whose contents are unproven: if retention reaches the top quartile it produces hobby income, if it reaches the top decile with hundreds of thousands of installs it produces a salary, and the model itself does little to change which of those happens.

Three readings matter for Pixel Pitch. The first is that **Retro Goal, not Retro Bowl, is the realistic reference**. Retro Bowl's breakout followed an App Store feature timed to the NFL season in September 2021 and heavy play in US schools that the studio thought "could be correlated" with TikTok, and its founder said the success "has taken us completely by surprise" ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/uk-based-new-star-games-takes-us-by-storm-with-retro-bowl/)). American football has few mobile competitors; football has dozens. The same studio, the same art style and the same model applied to soccer landed two orders of magnitude lower.

The second is that the pack-opening audience is large but attached to real players. MADFUT is parasitic on EA's Ultimate Team card data and restarts each year; a fictional-roster game cannot borrow the "pull a superstar" motive and should not assume MADFUT-scale installs. Pokémon TCG Pocket proves the free-timed-packs loop at enormous scale ($1.3bn in its first year), but it rests on the strongest collecting IP in the world and still fell from roughly $40-45m a month in 2025 to about $25m in the first half of 2026 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-tcg-pocket-makes-16bn-in-15-years/); [Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-pokemon-tcg-pocket-sled-surfers-my-hero-academia-maplestory-midcore-stats-and-more/)). Collection desire in Pixel Pitch has to come from art, rarity and album completion, and new sets are the main retention lever.

The third is that licences matter for revenue more than for downloads. In the World Cup month of July 2026 the two most-downloaded football games were unlicensed: Soccer Superstar at 14.2m and Football League 2026 at 12.4m, ahead of FC Mobile and eFootball ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/july-2026-mobile-game-charts/)). Tournaments lift downloads far more than money: AppMagic's benchmark is sports-game downloads up 54% across a World Cup and 71% at the finals, against revenue up 24% ([Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/world-cup-2026-games-trends/)), and the 2026 football titles all dropped off after the final on 19 July ([Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/julys-top-mobile-game-downloads-roblox-free-fire-arrows-block-blast-soccer-superstar-more/)). Pixel Pitch has missed that window. The next dated hooks are each August's season start and Euro 2028 in the UK and Ireland.

A genuine gap does exist. No competitor found combines pack collection, idle income, auto-simulated matches and pixel art in portrait, without ads and without dependence on EA's data. MADFUT 26's critical UK reviews ask for bulk pack opening, a quicker way to dispose of duplicates and safe trading ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/madfut-26/id6752884808)), all cheap for a newcomer to get right. The limits of this analysis are real, though: paywalled dashboards were not available, no systematic review mining was done, and no data was found for PacyBits, Score! Match, New Star Soccer, Pixel Cup Soccer or Football Chairman.

## Players spend about $7 each, and only 1 to 5 in 100 spend at all

No trustworthy public 2025-2026 table of ARPDAU by genre exists, and nothing specific to sports, card or idle games was found. The published ranges are undated vendor figures: roughly $0.01-0.05 per daily active user for ads-only casual games and $0.15-0.50 for hybrid casual with IAP ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/)). Revenue per install is the safer unit, and four dated anchors bracket it.

The first anchor is conversion. AppsFlyer's Q3 2026 benchmarks show the share of installs that purchase within 30 days at **4.93% on US iOS and 3.22% on US Android for sports games, 3.73% on UK Android**, against roughly 2.6% to 2.9% for all games in the US and UK ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/?p=549948)). These are paid-media cohorts from publishers running full economies, so they are a ceiling for a pass-and-gems indie. The second is spend per payer: casual games show a day-90 IAP ARPPU of $7.26 and midcore $9.80, with about half of first-time payers buying again within 30 days ([GameDev Reports summary of AppsFlyer](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appsflyer-app-monetization-in-2026); [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arppu-mobile-games)). Seven dollars is one to one-and-a-half season passes, which is what a pass-led payer plausibly buys in three months. The third is revenue per install: casual games earn a cumulative $1.34 of IAP and $0.55 of ad revenue per install by day 90 in the same AppsFlyer data, a figure skewed towards larger, marketed studios. The fourth is the indie reality check: AppMagic's 2021 figures imply about $0.16 per download for Retro Goal and about $0.88 for Retro Bowl (this report's division, and Retro Bowl's falls to about $0.43 on the studio's own 7M download count), and RevenueCat's 2026 data puts freemium apps at $0.27 per install in the first two weeks ([AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/retro-bowl-retro-goal); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)).

Platform and geography swing the numbers more than genre does. Sensor Tower's 2025 totals imply about $6.73 of IAP per App Store download against $0.71 on Google Play, roughly nine times, although much of that gap is country mix ([GameDev Reports summary of Sensor Tower](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)). North America and Europe run 20% to 25% above the global ARPU, mostly through higher conversion and not higher spend per payer ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arppu-mobile-games)). At the other extreme, Score! Hero's weekly revenue in Nigeria peaked at $152 against 98,000 to 130,000 active users ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-unified-top-5-soccer%20games-units-ng-60129cb7241bc16eb8b7b23c)). Football's audience is global and Android-heavy, which is the likeliest reason Retro Goal monetised at under a fifth of Retro Bowl per download.

On ads, US rewarded video pays roughly $13 to $17 per thousand impressions and Europe about $9 on iOS and $5 on Android in Appodeal's Q4 2024 data; no UK-specific level is published ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)). The ratio of casual ad revenue to IAP revenue per install in AppsFlyer's data is about 0.41, and Sensor Tower's hybrid-casual split for sports and racing is about 71% IAP to 29% ads ([Global Games Forum](https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/state-of-gaming-heres-what-the-mobile-data-actually-says)). Both point to rewarded ads adding **roughly 30% to 40% on top of IAP revenue**, an inference from those ratios and not a measured uplift. Vendor claims of 30% to 66% ARPDAU gains exist, and so does AppsFlyer's warning that rewarded ads can cannibalise IAP ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/rewarded-ad-benchmarks-2026); [AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/app-marketing-monetization-report-2024/)).

Spend concentration data is old: in 2015 the top 10% of payers produced 64% of revenue, and an academic study of 2020-2022 data found only about 12% of games show the extreme pattern where 1% of players generate 40% of revenue ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arppu-mobile-games)). The market backdrop is flat: worldwide mobile game IAP fell 2% year on year to $40bn in the first half of 2026 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-gaming-iap-dipped-2-as-ad-spend-climbed-in-h1-2026/)), while UK mobile game spend reached about £2.07bn in 2025 per Ukie ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/uk-games-market-hit-record-8-8bn-in-2025/)). No dated figure for average monthly spend per paying mobile gamer in the UK was found; the last one, from Deloitte in 2019, was about £3.59 a month ([Deloitte UK](https://www2.deloitte.com/uk/en/pages/press-releases/articles/mobile-gamers-spending-more-than-40-pounds-a-year-on-in-app-purchases.html)).

## Revenue scenarios: about £4 a week, £55 a week or £1,600 a week

Everything in this section is **this report's own modelling**. The inputs are chosen from the benchmarks above; the outputs are arithmetic, not forecasts. The formula is deliberately simple so the developer can change any input:

**Net revenue = installs × payer rate × spend per payer × (1 − store fee) − RevenueCat fee.**

Revenue is counted excluding VAT. Sterling figures use an assumed rate of **$1 = £0.75**, which is a planning assumption and not a sourced November 2026 rate; replace it with the day's rate. The store fee is 15% on both platforms, which is what a small developer pays: Apple's Small Business Program for developers under $1M a year ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/?page=2)) and, in the UK, US and EEA since 30 June 2026, Google's 10% service fee plus 5% for using Play Billing ([Android Developers Blog](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/expanded-billing-choice-and-lower-fees-on-google-play?hl=en)). RevenueCat is free below $2,500 of monthly tracked revenue and 1% above it ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing/)), so it applies only in the high case.

| Assumption (year one) | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Installs in the year | 5,000 | 25,000 | 300,000 |
| What that install level represents | Organic search and friends only | Steady store search, some community and short-video traction | A store feature or viral moment; still below the 500,000 that 1.3% of games reach |
| Retention (day 1 / day 7 / day 30) | 22% / 4% / 0.8% (GameAnalytics median) | 30% / 6.5% / 1.7% (top quartile) | 30-33% / 10-13% / 4-6% (AppsFlyer sports paid cohorts; day 30 extrapolated) |
| iOS share of installs | 30% | 45% | 55% |
| Installs that pay within 30 days | 1.0% | 2.5% | 4.5% |
| Spend per payer (ex-VAT) | $6 (one pass, little else) | $7.26 (AppsFlyer casual day-90 ARPPU) | $10 (about two passes, or a pass and gems) |
| Gross revenue per install | $0.06 | $0.18 | $0.45 |
| Store fee | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| RevenueCat | 0% | 0% | 1% of gross |
| Ads | None | None | None |

The retention rows come from GameAnalytics and AppsFlyer ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game); [AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/?p=549948)). The payer rates bracket AppsFlyer's all-games figure (about 2.8%) and sports figures (3.2% to 4.9%). The base revenue per install of $0.18 lands almost exactly on Retro Goal's estimated $0.16, which is the main reason to trust it as a centre. The iOS share is a guess that rises with scenario because a feature or a UK and US skew brings more iPhone users; the blended revenue per install can be rebuilt from it using roughly $0.33 per iOS install and $0.06 per Android install in the base case (an assumed ratio of about 5.5 to 1 within similar countries).

| Output | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross revenue per year | $300 | $4,500 | $135,000 |
| Net per year after fees | $255 (£191) | $3,825 (£2,869) | $113,400 (£85,050) |
| Net per month (average) | $21 (£16) | $319 (£239) | $9,450 (£7,088) |
| Net per week (average) | $5 (£4) | $74 (£55) | $2,181 (£1,636) |
| Implied average daily players | about 35 | about 300 | about 8,000 |
| Implied revenue per daily player | about $0.02 | about $0.04 | about $0.05 |
| If rewarded ads add 35% to gross | about $400 a year | about $6,100 a year | about $182,000 a year |

The daily-player rows are a cross-check built on a further assumption of 2.5, 4.5 and 10 lifetime active days per install; they show the model implies spend per daily player of two to five US cents, at or below the bottom of the published hybrid-casual range, so the scenarios are not flattered by aggressive monetisation assumptions. Weekly and monthly figures are simple averages. Real revenue will be front-loaded around launch and any feature, and will decay when installs slow, as AppMagic observed for both Retro titles.

How these compare with base rates is the candid part. RevenueCat's 2026 dataset of 115,000 apps shows the middle half earning $16 to $429 a month one year after launch, gaming's 75th percentile at $552 a month, and only 4.6% of new apps (9% in gaming) reaching $10,000 of monthly recurring revenue within two years ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)). MWM's April 2026 catalogue of about 44,000 monetising mobile games has a median of $462 a month, with about 59% under $1,000 ([MWM](https://mwm.ai/glossary/mobile-game-monetization)). So the base case at $375 gross a month is a typical-to-good outcome, not a pessimistic one; the low case is where most launches without distribution end up; and the high case sits in roughly the top twentieth. In this report's judgement the most likely first-year result lies between low and base. A Retro Bowl-style breakout is possible and deliberately not modelled, because it cannot be planned.

Costs change the picture at the bottom. Vercel Pro is $20 a month before usage ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/pro-plan)), so hosting alone roughly equals the low case's entire net revenue. Infrastructure stops mattering quickly: on this report's sizing it stays in the low single-digit percent of revenue from a few thousand daily players upward. The binding cost throughout is the developer's time, and a 28-day season is a content deadline every four weeks.

Pricing is the cheapest lever in the model. The League Pass has no price yet; the mobile norm is $5 to $15 a month, and a casual position argues for the bottom of that band. A £4.99 purchase by a UK player nets about £3.53 after 20% VAT and a 15% store fee on the VAT-exclusive price, about 71% of the sticker price (this report's worked example; the VAT ordering is supported by a developer forum post for Apple and is unverified for Google) ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/699667)).

## The factory makes siblings cheap to build and no easier to sell

The shared monorepo, the `create:app` template and pure per-game rules packages do what they promise: a second game reuses authentication, purchases, the backend factory, the game kit and telemetry, and needs new rules, art, copy, a catalogue and a store listing. That lowers the cost of the part of a sibling that was already cheapest. The costs that do not share are per title: art, store listing and keyword work, balance, legal review of a new sport's rights landscape, support, a season-pass content calendar, and above all finding an audience, in a market where downloads fell about 12% to 24bn in the first half of 2026 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/h1-2026-digital-gaming-market-index)).

The evidence on whether an audience transfers between sports is thin and discouraging. The one direct analogue is New Star Games: at the single comparable point in November 2021, Retro Goal had **about 2% of Retro Bowl's revenue and 13% of its downloads** ([AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/retro-bowl-retro-goal)). What the studio did next is telling. It went deeper into the sport that worked, with Retro Bowl College in 2023 and NFL-licensed annual editions on Apple Arcade from 2024 ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/nfl-retro-bowl-25-new-nfl-licensed-arcade-game-releasing-ahead-of-2024-season)), and did not spread across more sports. Template portfolios do succeed at volume, but with one title carrying the rest: Idle Miner Tycoon accounts for over 150 million of Kolibri's 250 million downloads ([Kolibri Games](https://kolibrigames.com/press/kolibri-games-surpasses-250-million-downloads-milestone/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/idle-miner-tycoon-hits-150-million-downloads)), each Kolibri sibling added new mechanics, and a typical long-tail tycoon such as Codigames' University Empire Tycoon earns under $10K a month on about 3M installs ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.codigames.university.empire.idle.tycoon)). Hyper-casual studios that live by this method report 3 to 40-plus prototypes per hit and kill the rest on early metrics ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/77158/tummy-games-nemanjua-divjak-hypercasual-voodoo/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-voodoo-approach-tarboosh-games/)). No published evidence was found that cross-promotion between sibling sports games moves installs.

Store policy is the hard constraint. Apple's guideline 4.3(a) uses this exact case as its example: "If your app has different versions for specific locations, sports teams, universities, etc., consider submitting a single app and providing the variations using in-app purchase", and 4.3(b) warns that repeated variant submissions can lead to removal from the developer programme ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Google's spam policy prohibits "creating multiple apps with highly similar functionality, content, and user experience" ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en)). Developer reports of 4.3 rejections describe multiple failed resubmissions even after substantial changes ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/843096)). A sibling that keeps the identical pack, lineup and idle shell and swaps sprites and nouns is close to the literal prohibited case. The series that coexist on the stores differ in core mechanics; Retro Goal has a different match engine from Retro Bowl. For Pixel Pitch that means a sport-specific simulation in the rules package and a different mini-game set, at minimum.

Which theme to pick is also less obvious than it looks. Baseball and golf earn an order of magnitude more per download than casual football (MLB 9 Innings reported $250M on about 26M downloads) ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mlb-9-innings-achieves-quarter-of-a-billion-in-lifetime-sales/)), but that spending is tied to real-player licences and older US, Japanese and Korean audiences. Cricket has huge, India-weighted installs and low spend. The only sports where an unlicensed casual title has demonstrably worked are American football and football. For non-sport themes, no evidence was found that an unlicensed fantasy, monster or racing re-skin monetises a pack loop better than sport; pack-opening earns when the cards mean something to the buyer before the game exists.

So the factory is scalable in the engineering sense and should be treated as a cheap option, not a growth plan. Re-skinning an unproven loop multiplies an unknown, and two live games with season passes means two content calendars for one person. Three uses of the factory look better than "the same game with a different ball": an adjacent football product once Pixel Pitch proves itself (a tournament or women's-game edition ahead of Euro 2028, or a platform deal), a single multi-sport app that sidesteps guideline 4.3 at the cost of separate keyword surface, and small standalone web games that reuse the mini-game kit and feed the main app.

## Capacitor is the right call; billing still starts with Apple and Google

### One codebase on Capacitor

For a UI-heavy card and idle game built by a web developer, one React codebase wrapped with Capacitor is the correct approach. Capacitor publishes an official games guide ([Capacitor docs](https://www.capacitorjs.com/docs/guides/games)), the main 2D web engine publishes its own Capacitor guide ([Phaser](https://phaser.io/tutorials/bring-your-phaser-game-to-ios-and-android-with-capacitor)), and RevenueCat ships an official Capacitor SDK ([RevenueCat docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/getting-started/installation/capacitor)). The alternatives win on action-game frame rates and on advertising SDK coverage, neither central to this game, and each costs a partial or full rewrite and the loss of the web build. That comparison is this report's assessment; the comparison sources found were vendor blogs, and no independent benchmark or verified list of commercial Capacitor hits was found.

Three risks deserve early tests. Performance on cheap Android phones is the real unknown: individual developers report white screens, texture failures and canvas warnings in Android WebViews ([Phaser forum](https://phaser.discourse.group/t/phaser-3-capacitor-android-apk-white-screens-texture-rendering-failures-and-orientation-issues/15571)), so the three canvas mini-games should be run on a three-to-four-year-old budget handset for frame rate, sound latency and memory before launch. Apple's guideline 4.2 requires an app to be more than "a repackaged website" ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)); a game with bundled assets, offline play, native purchases and push is not that, provided the store build never loads its UI from a remote URL. And Google requires new personal developer accounts to run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 continuous days before production access ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)); third-party sources say organisation accounts are exempt, which is worth confirming before the account is created. The case for leaving Capacitor would be measured frame drops in the mini-games, not a general principle. If ads are added, mediation support on Capacitor is thinner than on Unity by reputation; that was not verified and should be checked before committing.

### Billing by jurisdiction, November 2026

In-app sales of gems and the pass still go through Apple's and Google's billing by default, which is what RevenueCat wraps. Apple's rule is unchanged in principle: in-game currencies and unlocks "must use in-app purchase" ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). What changed between December 2025 and October 2026 is that selling on the developer's own website and crediting the player's account is now a recognised second channel, with rules that differ by platform and country. Apple allows web-bought consumables to appear in the app "provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app", and allows email, social and website promotion of other purchase methods everywhere ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

| Market | iOS: may the app point to a web shop? | Android: may the app point to a web shop? |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Yes, with no Apple commission for now. The Supreme Court agreed in June 2026 to hear Apple's appeal, with a ruling expected by June 2027, and the appeals court has said Apple may charge a cost-based commission ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/supreme-court-apple-epic-contempt-app-store-commission)) | Yes; Google's service fee applies to linked purchases ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)) |
| EU / EEA | Yes, under terms effective 1 October 2026: IAP 26% (15% small business), alternative in-app payment 20% (10%), link-out 15% (10%); chosen options are locked for 12 months ([Apple Newsroom](https://apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union)) | Yes; 10% service fee on the first $1M, plus 5% only if Play Billing is used ([Android Developers Blog](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/expanded-billing-choice-and-lower-fees-on-google-play?hl=en)) |
| United Kingdom | No. The CMA consulted in June 2026 on requirements to allow steering and promised a decision "later this year"; no final decision was found as of 25 November 2026 ([CMA](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-consults-on-new-requirements-for-apple-and-googles-mobile-platforms)) | Yes, since 30 June 2026; same 10% plus 5% structure as the EEA ([Android Developers Blog](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/expanded-billing-choice-and-lower-fees-on-google-play?hl=en)) |
| Japan | Yes; 15% on purchases made on a linked website ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/12/apple-announces-changes-to-ios-in-japan/)) | New fee model from 30 September 2026 ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)) |
| Rest of world | No in-app pointer; IAP only | Old schedule (15% on the first $1M, 30% above) until a rollout due by September 2027 ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/06/24/google-play-store-fee-change/)) |

The practical conclusion for a small developer is that separate billing is permitted in more places than it is worth using. On Android in the UK, keeping Play Billing costs 15%; switching to a web link costs 10% plus a card processor's fee, a saving of about two points in exchange for handling VAT, refunds and chargebacks. In the EU on iOS the gap between small-business IAP (15%) and a link-out (10%) is similar before processor fees. Only the US iOS position is materially better, and it is temporary by construction; no business plan should depend on a 0% commission. Web shops are now standard among the largest games (Bain estimates three-quarters of top-grossing mobile games run one) ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/game-distribution-half-your-players-already-buy-direct-gaming-report-2026/?linkId=1009147701)), but direct revenue is concentrated in genres with heavy spenders; casino leads and puzzle has a low share ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/sensor-tower-reveals-the-hidden-impact-of-d2c-revenue-in-mobile-games/)). Pixel Pitch's server-authoritative, account-based design already suits a web shop technically, so it can wait until revenue justifies it. These rules moved several times in 2026; the Google fee tiers and the VAT treatment should be confirmed in Play Console and with an accountant before modelling in detail.

## Ranked next moves: measure first, monetise second, multiply last

The ranking below orders moves by impact relative to effort for a solo developer. Effort and impact ratings are this report's judgement.

| Rank | Move | Effort | Impact | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soft launch and measure retention | Low | Decisive | Every later decision depends on day 1, day 7 and day 30 |
| 2 | Set prices and make the pass buyable in week one | Low | High | 60% of day-60 IAP revenue arrives by day 7 |
| 3 | Add opt-in rewarded ads | Medium | High | Roughly 30-40% on top of IAP; the niche's proven revenue stream |
| 4 | Featuring pitch, in-app events, pack-reveal short video, launch timed to the football calendar | Low to medium | High variance | The only zero-budget routes with documented step changes |
| 5 | Budget-Android performance test and store account setup | Low | Protective | Removes the main technical and process risks |
| 6 | Deepen gem sinks and collection content | Medium, recurring | Medium | Gems are the only uncapped line; new sets drive retention |
| 7 | Standalone web mini-game on a portal | Medium | Uncertain | Reuses the game kit; needs ads to earn |
| 8 | Web shop | Medium | Low at this scale | Saves a few points on a small base |
| 9 | Second title | Medium to high | Low until the first is proven | 2% sibling precedent; guideline 4.3 |
| 10 | Paid acquisition | Low to test | Negative at scale | Lifetime value far below install cost |
| 11 | Publisher | High | Conditional | Needs top-decile metrics; pays a share of net after recoup |
| Never, for now | Turn on paid random packs | Trivial | Negative | PEGI 16, odds disclosure, Belgium |

**Soft launch comes first because the thresholds are knowable and cheap to test.** Top-quartile retention is just over 30% on day 1 and 6% to 7% on day 7; top-decile is about 40% and 11% to 12% ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). A reasonable reading, and this report's inference, is that day 1 under 30% or day 7 under 6% means fixing the core loop before any marketing; 30-40% and 6-11% is a viable hobby-scale game; 40% and 12% or better is the level at which ads, a publisher or paid acquisition become worth serious effort. A few hundred installs per cohort is enough to tell a top-decile game from a median one. Practitioners now advise matching the test market to the monetisation model and starting with English-speaking countries for feedback ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/soft-launch-is-changing-in-2026-how-and-where-should-you-release-your-game/)). A UK and Ireland soft launch on organic and community traffic costs nothing but flatters the numbers; at sports install costs of about $0.17 to $0.30 in Brazil, Indonesia or Thailand, 1,000 Android installs for an unflattered read costs roughly $170 to $300, against about $2,900 in the UK ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/?p=549948)).

**Rewarded ads rank third, after measurement and pricing, because they are the largest known addition to revenue and the placements already exist.** They should be opt-in only, which keeps the "fair" positioning and suits a game that children will plausibly play. The caveats are the cannibalisation warning and unverified mediation support on Capacitor.

**Free distribution is where the variance is.** Apple takes featuring nominations through App Store Connect with two weeks' to three months' notice and publishes no criteria ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured)). Old studies put the uplift from a Game of the Day feature at about 800% for the week ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/04/20/ios-11s-new-app-store-boosts-downloads-by-800-for-featured-apps)), and one indie reported about 4,500 UK downloads on the day ([Medium](https://medium.com/jaysquared-com/my-experience-getting-featured-as-game-of-the-day-in-the-apple-app-store-d7e8e7992656)): useful momentum, not a business. Half of games now run in-app events ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/aso-app-store-trends-benchmarks-report)), and the 28-day season maps onto them directly. Pack opening is naturally short-video-shaped, and posting the game's own reveal clips daily costs only time; the base rate of a breakout is unknown and low. Lancaric advises against a global launch in the fourth quarter, which with the football calendar points to a pitch tied to the August 2027 season start.

**Paid acquisition does not pay on any benchmark found.** Sports installs cost $2.87 on UK Android and $7.27 on US iOS ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/?p=549948)), against this model's $0.18 to $0.45 of gross revenue per install, and even AppsFlyer's casual day-90 figure of about $1.89 with ads returns only around two-thirds of the UK Android cost. Buying tier-1 users profitably needs lifetime value of $2.50 to $4. The one sensible paid line is a few pounds a month of Apple Search Ads on the brand term and narrow discovery keywords, where the benchmark cost per tap is $0.64 ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)).

**A publisher is a conversation to have with data, or not at all.** Publishers gate on marketability tests (Homa's hybrid-casual bar is an install cost under $1.00 with deep early playtime) ([Homa](https://support.homagames.com/knowledge/how-to-read-the-results-of-a-test)) and typically pay about half of net revenue after recouping acquisition spend, which with thin lifetime value can mean no net at all ([Gamezebo](https://www.gamezebo.com/news/the-economics-of-mobile-game-publishing/); [GameAnalytics](https://gameanalytics.com/?p=258)). UK public money is a better-fitting source before global launch: the UK Games Fund's prototype grants cover up to 50% of the remaining budget and exclude fully launched games ([UK Games Fund](https://www.ukgamesfund.com/prototype-fund/)), and the Video Games Expenditure Credit is 34% of qualifying spend but needs a company and BFI certification ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec084000)). Whether a solo founder's own time counts towards either was not confirmed.

**The web portal and web shop are both real but second-order.** Poki has over 90 million monthly players and pays developers 50% on players it brings ([Poki](https://poki.com/blog/how-emolingo-games-built-business-html5-web-games-poki); [Poki](https://sdk.poki.com/deals)), and Retro Bowl runs an official web version there. Portals reward ad-funded, self-contained games, so the fit is a standalone penalty or pack-opening mini-game built from the game kit that points to the app, not the account-based club game itself. Whether portals permit outbound store links was not confirmed.

## Conclusion

The most useful shift in thinking is from "is the business model good?" to "is the game sticky?". The model is already close to the best available version of itself for a solo developer in 2026: cheap to run, fair to players, compliant with rules that tightened sharply this year, and compatible with every later upgrade (ads, web shop, more sinks). None of that determines the outcome. The spread between this report's low and high cases is about 450 times, and almost all of it comes from two inputs the model does not control: how many people install, and whether they are still playing in week four. The first is largely luck plus persistence in free channels. The second is measurable for a few hundred dollars before launch, and it also decides whether the app factory is an asset: a proven loop is worth re-theming carefully, and an unproven one is not.

The implication for the factory is the uncomfortable one. Its value is not that it produces many games, since the stores discourage that and the one precedent says siblings do not inherit an audience. Its value is that it makes each experiment cheap enough that the developer can afford to be wrong, and can go deeper on football the moment the numbers say it is working, with Euro 2028 on home soil as the natural deadline.
